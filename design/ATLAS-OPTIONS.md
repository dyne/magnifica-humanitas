## Grounded structural candidates

Ordered by resonance with that scene; all keep the complete atlas and HTML
alternative, source context, continuous reading and secondary trails.

1. **Atlas beside the reader.** A concept neighborhood and the selected article
   share the desktop; on phones selection leads into a full-width article with
   a compact neighborhood above its connections. Strong continuity; dense map
   requires careful simplification on phones.
2. **Question regions.** Human questions organize the overview into labelled
   regions; choosing one exposes its concept neighborhood and then the reader.
   Strong invitation; grouping must be visibly editorial rather than a source
   taxonomy or a second mandatory navigation step.
3. **Relationship index.** A readable edge table and a diagram share the overview;
   selection opens a relationship and its two concepts. Strong explanation;
   potentially abstract before a reader knows a concept.
4. **Reading spine with atlas margin.** A sequential adapted section is positioned
   beside its conceptual connections. Strong long-form depth; less direct for
   readers seeking a personally relevant starting idea.
5. **Junction atlas.** The overview supplies starting concepts; each selection
   becomes a large, still junction with named outgoing and incoming connections.
   Read the selected idea, then choose the next connection or open its full
   reasoning. Strong one-decision orientation; relationships outside the active
   neighborhood require returning to the overview.
6. **Two-concept workbench.** Choose a concept pair and inspect the relation,
   distinctions and source evidence alongside their full explanations. Strong
   comparison; choosing a pair adds a task before entry.
7. **Source-window atlas.** A paragraph-context window accompanies the concept
   map, revealing how a selected edge is evidenced. Strong traceability;
   introduces source apparatus early for readers unaccustomed to long texts.
