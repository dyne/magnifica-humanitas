# French source alignment and authoring handoff

`alignment-review.json` records L6.1 review against the frozen official French
edition, `mh-fr-official-html-2026-10-14`, bound to its HTML hash. All 245 complete
paragraphs and all 224 local notes were read. The public reading and interface
are L6.2 and L6.3 work. This review makes no native-speaker proofreading,
external factual updating, reader-study or learning-efficacy claim.

The 27-term map supplies French display wording and separate exact local source
anchors. Use **IA**, **dignité humaine**, **bien commun**, **destination universelle
des biens**, **subsidiarité**, **solidarité**, **justice sociale** and **développement
humain intégral** consistently. **Soin et proximité humaine** captures the care
argument without restricting it to clinical treatment. **Corps et finitude** and
**Liberté et capacité d’agir** are explanatory editorial labels. §105 calls the
accountability requirement *responsabilité*, explaining the ability to identify
who must *rendre compte*, give reasons, undergo control and challenge, and repair
damage. The label **Rendre compte** distinguishes this from generic responsibility.
§86 uses the spelling *rendre-compte*. Do not import the Spanish *accountability*
loanword into an apparent French source quotation.

Nine relation labels preserve the directed editorial types. Their anchors explain
the kind of reasoning involved; the display verbs themselves are editorial labels,
not purported quotations or statistical causal claims. Foundations relations stay
optional and attributed. Concepts and relationships retain their stable identities.

## Coverage comparison and boundaries

The coverage review retains 137 retain, 66 adapt and 42 optional-context decisions;
none omits a whole paragraph. Each has its own French paragraph hash and internal
review instruction. The accepted policy instructions were compared with the full
French paragraphs and retained where their meaning applies; edition-specific
instructions were rewritten for French. They are English internal guidance,
not public French prose. They must never be copied into a reading explanation.

Eighty-one optional faith-premise records retain full local paragraph contexts
and attributed source annotations. Full-paragraph contexts are review boundaries;
they do not authorize publishing mixed devotional passages or mechanical slicing.
Eighteen ceremonial/devotional forms have individually located French UTF-16
coordinates, full paragraph contexts, span/paragraph hashes and preservation
instructions. Seventy protected spans bind the qualifications and surrounding
arguments. Whole blessings, invocations and responses remain global exclusions.
Only §241's incomplete coordinated **prière et** has reviewed `source-location`
matching: it belongs to the final exhortation, while Néhémie's prayer earlier in
that paragraph remains reported narrative. Never splice surviving fragments into
a quotation.

The substantive comparison found the following requirements for the next child:

- §§1–16: preserve the generational choice of humane construction, technical
  benefits and uncertain effects, private transnational power, plurality, shared
  responsibility and practical criteria. Babel and Néhémie are attributed biblical
  images; creation, Incarnation and Christian hope are premises available in
  foundations. §16 retains planning, persistent work and rejected people after
  separating direct prayer and blessing.
- §§17–45: the Church's public mission accompanies earthly autonomy and distinct
  civil duties. Emergency service cannot permanently replace civil institutions.
  Dialogue with science includes diverse opinions and no claim to a final answer
  on every question. Historical development is the author's account. Intolerance
  and violence in truth's name (§25), critique of totalitarianism, social property
  limits, worker association, qualified appraisal of democracy and markets, and
  non-linear development remain intelligible. The history is not independent
  certification of every historical assertion.
- §§46–64: the principles illuminate one another. §52 separates moral conduct,
  social treatment and existential self-perception, which can change, from
  ontological dignity, which cannot be lost through sin, failure, humiliation
  or exclusion. The divine-love premise remains attributed. Rights require
  practical and legal protection. §55 retains the Church's conception-to-natural-end,
  induced-abortion, killing-of-innocents and euthanasia positions. Women's equal
  rights require laws, education, work, public responsibility and attention to
  their contribution. The common good exceeds summed private advantages; State
  coordination includes the weakest. §63 criticizes short-term calculations and
  opposition rather than explicitly naming corruption. §64 rejects eliminating
  or subjugating nations and respects their legitimate plurality.
- §§65–89: private property is recognized but subordinated to universal goods.
  §67 extends the question to patents, algorithms, platforms, infrastructure and
  data, and requires environmental regulation against **gaspillage** and **pillage**;
  Spanish **estafa** is not French wording. Subsidiarity requires support and
  coordination, expressly rejects State disengagement (§69), and reaches corporate
  power with independent controls, transparency, access and recourse. Participation
  precedes settled decisions. Chosen solidarity exceeds factual connectivity;
  justice changes structures and repairs inherited harms. Migrants need safe legal
  routes, integration and the right to remain safely at home. Church transparency,
  actual participation, all six named abuse categories, listening, acknowledgment,
  reparation, prevention and corrective assessment stay explicit. Eucharistic
  solidarity is a substantive theological rationale, not a prayer to discard.
- §§90–111: stronger capabilities are not necessarily better development. §97
  limits scope; §98 warns of rapid obsolescence and limited knowledge of systems.
  §99 acknowledges concrete benefits alongside categorical denials of experience,
  embodiment and conscience: these are Léon XIV's claims, not scientific consensus.
  Simulated relational language can help yet can weaken desire for real human
  company. Environmental costs and political effects do not disappear behind
  convenience. Choices enter data, objectives and models before use. Identifiable
  responsibility, legal frameworks, independent control, sometimes slower adoption,
  public deliberation of values, regulated data ownership and common/collective
  management exceed a technical alignment exercise. Justice starts in design.
  Disarming IA is a political and ecological metaphor, not rejection of technology.
- §§112–130: do not reduce diverse enhancement currents to one extreme program;
  radical and speculative qualifications matter. Technology may support care
  without taking human freedom or judgement away. §118 says **it is our duty to
  try to eliminate suffering**, while acknowledging constitutive finitude. This
  conjunction never authorizes refusal of treatment or imposed avoidable pain.
  §120's claims about suffering, love and desire remain attributed philosophy,
  not clinical advice. Frankl's named prayers are testimony, not prayers performed
  for readers. Historical institutions and individual examples show fragile
  possibilities, not guaranteed progress. Grace is divine gift and transformation
  through relationship, distinct from technical self-enhancement.
- §§131–147: truth needs verification, source comparison, argument and trustworthy
  relationships. Power and visibility do not make facts. Journalism exposing
  Church wrongdoing complements, never replaces, the Church's own duty to face
  uncomfortable truths. Parents need public support against attention markets.
  §143 retains their **primordial and inalienable** educational-choice right and
  moral, cultural and religious convictions, with attribution. Access, pedagogy
  and intellectual wisdom are distinct school challenges. Patient dialogue and
  deciding when not to use IA matter; quick answers do not exhaust education.
- §§148–169: work expresses dignity without conferring intrinsic human worth.
  Emergency assistance can be necessary. Relief from arduous, repetitive or
  dangerous work is desirable; profit cannot systematically sacrifice people.
  Transitions vary by place and require adaptable responses; participation must
  survive absence of employment (§154). Anticipation, accessible training and
  worker participation are public and institutional duties. GDP needs complementary
  measures. Credit serving real development and jobs differs from finance for
  itself. Justice operates throughout production and redistribution. §165's
  stable man–woman family definition and original rights stay attributed and
  unbroadened; employment, services, rest and training affect family and youth
  stability rather than becoming blame directed at individuals.
- §§170–181: agency needs public safeguards and proportionate limits as well as
  interior freedom. Hidden data labour, material extraction, trafficking and
  colonial data control are distinct. §176 explicitly admits ecclesiastical
  slaveholding, pontifical regulation and legitimation, delayed condemnation and
  a wound in Christian memory, ending with pardon sought in the Church's name.
  Caution against anachronism does not cancel that admission. It is institutional
  accountability, not a prayer exclusion. §177 requires present prevention,
  protection, liberation and rehabilitation. §178 returns peoples' power to decide
  uses and benefits, rather than merely copies of their data. §179 names separate
  duties for supply chains, investors, platforms, authorities and civil society.
- §§182–208: dual-use ambiguity includes possible civilian protection but also
  easier resort to force and opaque responsibility. Military-industrial incentives,
  weakened memory and digital polarization prepare the culture of war. §192
  preserves legitimate defense in its strictest sense; §197 adds last resort.
  §198 forbids delegating lethal or otherwise irreversible decisions and positively
  calls for values and prudent judgement in programming, a moral ecosystem helping
  people heed conscience and appropriate model limits. §§199–200 retain identifiable
  chains, time for moral judgement, civilian distinction, reconstructability and
  effective, conscious, responsible human control. A nominal human button is
  insufficient. Protect civilians and survival infrastructure; do not make the
  text a tactical decision tool. The source's contemporary diagnoses remain dated
  and attributed rather than silently updated.
- §§209–228: researchers and institutions cannot hide behind narrow professional
  neutrality. People have responsibilities of unequal scope and power. Words can
  disarm without silencing denunciation. Operational humanitarian neutrality
  (§123) differs from indifference to attacks on civilians (§216). Memory gives
  victims recognition and supports peace rather than vengeance. Healthy realism
  seeks viable institutional change instead of selective facts or resignation.
  Diplomacy includes difficult interlocutors; multilateral institutions require
  deep ethical and structural reform. §228's unarmed, disarming, humble, persistent
  peace remains an attributed ethical ideal, with practical commitment in society,
  after removing performed blessing and calls to pray.
- §§229–245: the Christian itinerary, Incarnation and Eucharistic membership are
  substantive premises. Remove enacted responses while explaining truthful assent,
  unity and solidarity. §236 rejects spiritual retreat; education, physical care,
  hidden work, justice and participation remain concrete. Néhémie's narrative
  retains organization, courage and shared responsibility. §243 separates unchanged
  Roman domination from Mary's changed perspective; the social reversal and God's
  siding with the lowly remain attributed. §244 explicitly includes widows, orphans,
  strangers, wounded children, exiles and fugitives. §245 keeps everyday sharing
  of oneself and one's goods while separating final Marian entrustment. Public
  explanations must state these arguments directly without editorial-process
  instructions or repeated assembled theological appendices.

## Every local note and edition differences

All 224 local note markers, hashes, backlinks and explicit same-number mappings
were checked. Thirty-five **ibid.** references identify the preceding local note,
including n204 → n203 and n211 → n210. **Id.** changes/repeats an author and is
not by itself an instruction to repeat the previous work. n217 is an internal
reference to paragraphs 11–14. Exact citation titles may name prayer, blessings,
Mass or liturgy without performing a prayer.

Four notes contain argument beyond bibliography: **n132, n174, n175 and n182**.
n132 connects the heart with encounter, poetry and personal history; §120's
explanation must include that argument. n174 names the bulls and political and
economic pressures that subordinated evangelization; n175 records the 1866
Holy Office distinction between immoral and moral servitude rather than complete
condemnation. These deepen §176's institutional admission. n182 warns against
broad legitimate-defense and preventive-attack justifications, complementing §192.
French **n143 is bibliographic only**: Arendt's English *The Origins of
Totalitarianism*, III, New York 1962, page 474. It does not contain the German
edition-comparison argument; none may be imported into French §134.

The frozen text includes **famille,reconnaît** (§30), **commun.Il** (§32),
**réellement.Après** (§121), **NeuvièmeSymphonie** (§122) and **rend-elle«** (§129).
§176 uses **crée** and mixed apostrophes. §234 cites **1 Co 12, 17**. n089 prints
**AAS 107 (2025)** for *Laudato si’*; n213 dates *Deus caritas est* **25 décembre
2025** while citing **AAS 98 (2006)**. These source anomalies remain untouched;
quotations and note text must not silently repair them. This is a bounded witness
inventory, not an exhaustive typography audit.

French has 82 local heading blocks including h000, with its own chapter-group
structure and official fragment metadata. Structural correspondence does not
establish complete semantic equality or full HTML/PDF equality. Preserve accents,
punctuation, apostrophes and original Unicode normalization.

were computed from rendered authored prose at an assumed 200 words per minute:
**20, 27, 19, 21, 23 and 30 minutes**, in canonical trail order. These are estimates,
